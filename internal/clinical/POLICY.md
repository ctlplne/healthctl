# Development warning policy v1

These rules have not undergone clinical review or validation. They cover a narrow sample of wording. Their silence cannot establish that a user can wait or that an emergency has been excluded.

An affirmative user response to the explicit current emergency question immediately displays an application-controlled message. Selected current first-person descriptions of breathing difficulty, chest symptoms, bleeding, sudden neurologic symptoms or suicidal intent can raise the same interruption. The program also accepts a model-suggested warning with an exact active user quotation and checks its surrounding context. Neither a subsequent routine answer nor an assessment can dismiss a previously displayed warning. Correcting its source records a new history version and retains the warning and resolution in the report. Urgency has no numerical relationship to the ranking. A user can review and export an incomplete history and explicitly request an estimate for a confirmed, supported history with an active warning.

Source for the choice of example signs: [American College of Emergency Physicians, Know When to Go](https://www.emergencyphysicians.org/article/know-when-to-go/know-when-to-go-overview) and [US National Library of Medicine, Recognizing medical emergencies](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm). These references do not validate this software. English context matching only handles a subset of denials, uncertainty, timing and subject changes. It can miss urgent situations or interrupt unnecessarily. The initial tests are software regressions. Review of intended use, the actual rules, wording, sources and localized escalation pathways is needed before public real-world use.

## Development policy v2 and candidate interview pathways

Stored v1 warnings retain their original policy identifier. Version `development-2` adds selected sudden neurologic and severe abdominal symptom descriptions, with the same context checks and independent explicit emergency screen. Each of the four `adult-development-v2` pathways supplies topics for the interviewer based on the user's own current wording. Mixed concerns can match more than one pathway. The pathways cover respiratory symptoms, headache, abdominal symptoms and dizziness. They have not received professional review and do not establish urgency or exclude a diagnosis. [CDC stroke signs](https://www.cdc.gov/stroke/signs-symptoms/index.html) · [ACEP emergency symptom examples](https://www.emergencyphysicians.org/article/know-when-to-go/know-when-to-go-overview)

The upload screen locally checks for a printed critical flag or an explicit urgent instruction. An active report warning identifies the precise row and asks the reader to check the original and contact the issuing service. Conditional safety-net instructions, a routine H/L flag, a numeric result alone and a known third-party report do not trigger this rule. An uncertain OCR reading can produce a preliminary warning. Changing the owner or correcting the specific source retains the earlier warning record. Withdrawing permission to send a row never clears an unresolved warning.

The report-guided questions use locally reviewed entries and current user input. A dated relevant sample can raise a timing question. A disputed source, uncertain transcription or comparable same-date measurements can raise a clarification. The user may decline, stop, or choose review. The report demo illustrates a fictional urinary symptom and selected urine test row. Broader use needs clinical evaluation. [Understanding laboratory results](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/how-to-understand-your-lab-results/) · [NIDDK adult bladder infection symptoms](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/bladder-infection-uti-in-adults/symptoms-causes)

## Visible changes and development policy v3

User-reported new yellowing of the whites of the eyes can interrupt the interview with an urgent advice message. The warning uses the person's words, can remain active in an export, and takes priority over a request for a photo. Lighting and a photograph cannot establish or exclude jaundice. Nail distribution, progression, local injury, and usual activity are interview topics. An inward curve reported by a user does not establish a cause. These development rules need independent clinical review. [NHS jaundice](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/jaundice/) · [MedlinePlus nail abnormalities](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003247.htm)

## Development policy v4: one context engine and two warning levels

Version `development-4` replaces the earlier clause-wide checks with one context engine (`context.go`). The safety screen, the finding validator, pathway matching and contradiction checks all use it. These rules have not undergone clinical review. They are development content.

- **Scoped negation.** A word such as "no", "never" or "without" applies only to the words it governs, up to a clause break, so "I have chest pain and no fever" still warns. Lists such as "no fever, chills, or difficulty breathing" stay negated. Phrases like "isn't going away", "can't move my wrist without pain", "no appetite" and "not until today" are not denials.
- **Who the words are about.** In the user's own words, "my mother", "he" and "she" mean someone else. In a witness account, "he", "she" and "my wife" mean the user, and "I" means the witness.
- **Past, hypothetical and uncertain.** Remote history ("years ago", "as a child", "in 2019") raises no warning. A recent past event ("last week", "three days ago") raises a "check this" warning. A problem that is back now ("again", "came back", "still") counts as current. "What if", "in case" and "signs of" mark a hypothetical, but "What if this is a heart attack?" and "If I lie down I can't breathe" do not. Uncertainty never removes a warning.
- **Wider vocabulary.** The screen covers more ways to describe breathing difficulty, chest pain, stroke signs, fainting and seizures, suicidal thoughts and self-harm, overdose and poisoning, bleeding, severe allergic reactions, severe abdominal pain, sudden headache with a stiff neck or fever, and others. A plain "yes" to a question about one of these counts as reporting it.
- **Two warning levels.** "Seek local emergency care immediately" interrupts the interview. "Get this checked promptly" stays visible and lets the interview continue. It is used for recent past events, some signs that need a prompt check (such as a swollen calf), and model flags that the app's own reading disagrees with.
- **Model flags.** A model's urgent flag needs only an exact quote from a substantive user statement. It never fails a turn. The app chooses the level and the wording. A model can never clear a warning.
- **Every input.** Answers, corrections, diary saves, witness accounts, visual notes, report passages and review notes, dictation drafts, return-visit clarifications, reconciliation notes and outcomes all pass through the same screen.

The regression corpus in `testdata/corpus` (650 synthetic sentences) passes with no missed urgent sentence. Held-out sets written after the rules show the limits of keyword rules: before tuning, the rules missed 24 of 34 urgent sentences in the first held-out set and 20 of 27 in the second. Silence from these rules never means a person can wait. The explicit emergency question and the model's flags remain essential, and clinical review is still required.

## Development policy v5: red-flag questions, completeness and question intents

Version `development-5` and pathway version `adult-development-v4` move more of the interview under the application's control. All of this is development content. It has not been clinically reviewed.

- **Red-flag questions.** For each active pathway the app asks its own safety questions before the history review: for example "Have you coughed up any blood?" for respiratory symptoms, or "Did this headache come on suddenly and severely, reaching its worst within about a minute?" for a headache. A yes raises the emergency warning without asking the model. "Not sure" is listed for the clinician. Any red flag still unanswered appears on the review screen and in the confirmed summary.
- **Completeness.** Each pathway lists what the app expects, such as onset, course, severity, character, location, triggers, prior episodes and medicine changes. The user's own words, or an earlier question, can already cover an element. The app asks the rest in its own wording before review. If the user stops early, the gaps stay listed in the app's words; the model cannot remove them.
- **Question intents.** The model names an intent from the application catalog (`intents.go`) and the app writes the question, the choices and the "Why this question?" text. A free-text question is a fallback with stricter checks, and its choices may not name a symptom or urgent sign the user hasn't reported.
- **Question budget.** The model may ask at most 12 questions in one encounter. After that its proposed question becomes an open item.
- **Safety questions while a warning is active.** Answering no or not sure to a red-flag question on the review screen keeps the review open even when an earlier emergency warning is active. A yes still raises a new warning and pauses the interview.
- **"They" in the user's own words** (`validator-3`). When the app checks the model's findings, "they" or "them" with no person named before it, in the answer or in the question it answers, is read as the user's own symptoms: "They have become more frequent" about headaches. A relative or a family question keeps it someone else's. Screening for warnings still reads a bare "they" as someone else. Development content, not clinically reviewed.
- **Concept vocabulary.** Findings are mapped to a plain term with an ICD-10-CM code where one is well established (`vocabulary.go`, `development-1`), so contradiction checks compare synonyms as one finding.
